## Practice Questions

Try answering before checking the key.

1. Why is the Constituent Assembly criticised as unrepresentative?
2. How were its members selected?
3. Distinguish voice representation from opinion representation.
4. Give two arguments used to defend the Assembly's representativeness.
5. Why should the 1951–52 election not be described as a formal referendum on the Constitution?
6. Write a 150-word answer: “The Constituent Assembly was not fully representative, yet its deliberative process gave the Constitution broader legitimacy.” Discuss.

## Answer Key

1. It was not elected directly by all adult Indians, and several social groups lacked direct physical representation.
2. Provincial representatives were indirectly elected by Provincial Legislative Assemblies; princely-state representatives were selected through arrangements with their rulers.
3. Voice representation means physical presence and direct articulation by group members; opinion representation means their concerns are considered even when they lack a dedicated representative.
4. Examples: public feedback on the draft, wide-ranging debates, and constitutional provisions addressing social justice and minority concerns.
5. Voters selected parties and candidates for many reasons; there was no ballot asking them to approve or reject the Constitution itself.
6. Include both the limitation of restricted electoral representation and the strengths of public consultation and deliberation.
