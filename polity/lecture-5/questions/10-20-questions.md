## Practice Questions
1. Explain the criticism that the Constituent Assembly was unrepresentative. What arguments qualify this criticism?
2. What is the difference between voice representation and opinion representation?
3. “The Constituent Assembly was Congress-dominated, but Congress was a broad church.” Discuss.
4. Does the Hindu-majority composition of the Constituent Assembly prove that the Constitution reflected Hindu religious norms? Explain critically.

## Answer Pointers
1. Limited franchise and indirect election; public consultation, inclusive debates and constitutional safeguards qualify—but do not erase—the criticism.
2. Voice representation is the presence of a group’s own representative; opinion representation is the inclusion of its concerns in debates and provisions.
3. Recognise Congress dominance, then discuss ideological diversity within Congress, committees, amendments and public feedback.
- The Congress was a broad political coalition rather than a party with one completely uniform ideology.
- It included people with different views and interests: socialists, liberals, secular nationalists, business interests, peasants’ and workers’ representatives, and others.
- Therefore, Congress membership alone does not prove that every constitutional provision reflected one narrow Congress ideology.
5. Separate the religious composition of members from the content of constitutional provisions; assess equality, religious freedom and minority safeguards while acknowledging representational gaps.
