## 5. Mains Practice Questions

### Question 1 — Criticism + Qualification

**Q1.** Explain the criticism that the idea of “basic features” is uncertain because there is no authoritative list. What arguments qualify this criticism?

**Model Answer (maximum 250 words)**

**Introduction:**  
The idea of basic features refers to characteristics identified as fundamental to the Constitution. The lecture distinguishes this descriptive exercise from the Basic Structure doctrine.

**Body**

**Criticism — No authoritative list:** The Constituent Assembly did not provide a definitive list of basic features.

**Criticism — Subjective selection:** Political writers and constitutional experts may identify different features depending on their perspectives.

**Qualification — Constitutional analysis:** The features are distilled from the Constitution by examining its provisions and overall design.

**Qualification — Separate doctrine:** The absence of a fixed list of basic features does not make the Basic Structure doctrine identical to that exercise; the lecture treats them as separate concepts.

**Conclusion:**  
The idea of basic features is not an official exhaustive list. It is an analytical description, and it must not be conflated with the distinct Basic Structure doctrine.

**Keywords to hit:** Basic features, Constituent Assembly, constitutional experts, interpretation, Basic Structure doctrine, distinction.

**Common mistake:** Claiming that the Constituent Assembly itself prepared an exhaustive list of basic features.

### Question 2 — Concept Contrast

**Q2.** What is the difference between the First Schedule and the Second Schedule of the Constitution?

**Model Answer (maximum 150 words)**

**Introduction:**  
The First and Second Schedules serve different supporting functions within the constitutional framework.

**Body**

- **First Schedule:** It lists the States and Union Territories and is linked to Articles 1 and 4.
- **Territorial detail:** It provides information associated with the formation and subsequent territorial changes of States.
- **Second Schedule:** It deals with salaries, emoluments, privileges and conditions of service of specified constitutional dignitaries.
- **Coverage:** The lecture lists the President, Vice-President, Governors, judges, CAG and legislative presiding officers among the offices covered.

**Conclusion:**  
The First Schedule supports the territorial provisions of the Constitution, while the Second Schedule deals with service-related matters of specified constitutional offices.

**Keywords to hit:** First Schedule, Second Schedule, Articles 1 and 4, States, Union Territories, emoluments.

**Common mistake:** Mixing the territorial list in the First Schedule with service conditions in the Second Schedule.

### Question 3 — Quoted Statement + Discuss

**Q3.** “A constitutional oath is a commitment to follow a general constitutional direction, but it also carries responsibilities specific to the office.” Discuss.

**Model Answer (maximum 150 words)**

**Introduction:**  
An oath or affirmation is a formal commitment made by a person assuming a constitutional office.

**Body**

**General commitment:** The oath contains a common direction that applies across constitutional positions.

**Office-specific commitment:** It also reflects the particular responsibilities associated with the office being assumed.

**Different forms:** The Constitution prescribes different oath forms because the duties and responsibilities of constitutional positions differ.

**Prescribed wording:** The Third Schedule includes the option to swear in the name of God or solemnly affirm.

**Conclusion:**  
An oath is not merely a ceremonial statement. It combines a general commitment with obligations relevant to the particular constitutional office.

**Keywords to hit:** Oath, affirmation, incumbent, general commitment, specific direction, Third Schedule.

**Common mistake:** Treating every constitutional oath as identical despite differences in the responsibilities of offices.

### Question 4 — Critical Evaluation

**Q4.** Does the option to “swear in the name of God” or “solemnly affirm” make the constitutional oath flexible without making it open-ended? Explain critically.

**Model Answer (maximum 250 words)**

**Introduction:**  
The Third Schedule provides an alternative between swearing in the name of God and solemnly affirming. The lecture examines the limits of variation in the prescribed oath.

**Body**

**Choice within the form:** The person taking the oath may choose either the religious wording or the secular affirmation.

**Language translation:** The lecture notes that equivalent words for God in the language used for the oath, such as Ishwar, Allah or Deva, have been accepted by courts.

**Limits on substitution:** The teacher distinguishes translation of the prescribed wording from replacing it with a different object, such as Gomatha, farmers or a spiritual personality.

**Constitutional form:** The central issue is whether the oath remains within the form prescribed by the Constitution.

**Conclusion:**  
The oath allows a choice between swearing and affirming, and permits equivalent linguistic expression as described in the lecture. That flexibility should not be treated as permission to replace the prescribed commitment with an unrelated one. The precise legal consequence of every deviation is not established by this transcript.

**Keywords to hit:** Third Schedule, swear, solemnly affirm, prescribed form, translation, constitutional oath.

**Common mistake:** Assuming that the choice to affirm permits any wording or object to be substituted.
