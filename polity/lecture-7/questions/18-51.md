## 4. Mains Practice Questions

### Q1. Explain the criticism that India's constitutional amendment process gives insufficient importance to the States. What features qualify this criticism?
**Word limit: 250**

**Intro:** India's amendment power is principally exercised by Parliament, but the Constitution requires State ratification for specified federal provisions.

**Body:**

- **Parliamentary dominance:** Most amendments are passed by Parliament; States do not participate in every amendment.
- **Federal safeguard:** Amendments affecting specified federal provisions require ratification by at least half of the State Legislatures.
- **Different threshold:** State Legislatures approve through simple-majority resolutions, while Parliament uses the prescribed special majority.
- **Limited scope:** State ratification applies to identified matters such as the President's election, Union–State powers and Seventh Schedule Lists.
- **Comparative qualification:** Unlike the US requirement described in the lecture, India does not require State ratification for every constitutional amendment.

**Conclusion:** The process is Union-dominated for most amendments, but preserves a federal safeguard where amendments directly affect the constitutional position of States.

**Keywords to hit:** Article 368, federal provisions, half of States, special majority, simple-majority resolution, federal safeguard.  
**Common mistake:** Claiming that every constitutional amendment requires ratification by State Legislatures.

### Q2. What is the difference between parliamentary supremacy, judicial supremacy and India's “via media” model?
**Word limit: 150**

**Intro:** These expressions describe the relative authority of legislatures and courts in a constitutional system.

**Body:**

- **Parliamentary supremacy:** The UK is traditionally associated with Parliament's dominant law-making authority; the lecture notes that primary legislation is not ordinarily struck down by courts.
- **Judicial supremacy:** In the US example, the Supreme Court can invalidate legislation inconsistent with the Constitution.
- **Via media:** India allows Parliament to respond to judicial decisions by changing laws or amending the Constitution.
- **Basic-structure limit:** Parliament cannot use its amending power to destroy the Constitution's basic structure.

**Conclusion:** India follows neither unlimited parliamentary supremacy nor unqualified judicial supremacy; it maintains a constitutional balance.

**Keywords to hit:** Parliamentary supremacy, judicial review, via media, amendment, basic structure, constitutional balance.  
**Common mistake:** Saying Parliament can overturn any Supreme Court judgment without constitutional limits.

### Q3. “Political equality is necessary, but it does not by itself produce substantive equality.” Discuss.
**Word limit: 150**

**Intro:** Political equality provides equal civic and electoral status, while substantive equality addresses disadvantages that persist in society.

**Body:**

**Limitation:** Formal equality does not remove the rich–poor divide, caste inequality or gender inequality. Equal treatment under law alone may leave unequal starting conditions unchanged.

**Qualification:**

- **Political justice:** Equality before law and universal adult franchise provide equal political status.
- **Social justice:** The Constitution seeks to address social disadvantage, including caste-based inequality.
- **Economic justice:** Measures such as reservations and benefits for disadvantaged groups aim to reduce unequal conditions.
- **Substantive equality:** The objective is not merely identical treatment, but more effective equality in practice.

**Conclusion:** Political equality is foundational, but social and economic justice are needed to make equality meaningful in lived conditions.

**Keywords to hit:** Political justice, formal equality, substantive equality, social justice, economic justice, universal adult franchise.  
**Common mistake:** Treating equality before law as proof that social and economic inequalities have disappeared.

### Q4. Does India's federal structure make the States fully autonomous? Explain critically.
**Word limit: 250**

**Intro:** Federalism involves multiple levels of government with defined powers and responsibilities, but the distribution of authority need not be perfectly equal.

**Body:**

- **Federal structure:** India has Union and State levels with defined spheres of action and responsibilities.
- **Autonomy:** Each level enjoys a degree of autonomy within its assigned sphere.
- **Unitary bias:** The lecture characterises India as a federal system with a stronger Centre.
- **Power distribution:** The Centre is described as having comparatively greater legislative, administrative and financial powers.
- **Emergency dimension:** Article 356 illustrates the Union's ability to intervene when a State government cannot be carried on in accordance with the Constitution.

**Conclusion:** India is federal in structure but not based on complete equality of power between the Union and States; its framework has a centralising tilt.

**Keywords to hit:** Federalism, Union–State relations, autonomy, unitary bias, legislative powers, Article 356.  
**Common mistake:** Describing India as either a purely unitary State or a federation with completely equal Union and State powers.
