# Constitutional Oaths and the Fourth, Fifth and Sixth Schedules — UPSC Notes

## 1. Topic + One-line Summary

**Constitutional oaths and selected Schedules:** Different constitutional offices have role-specific oaths; the Fourth Schedule allocates Rajya Sabha seats, while the Fifth and Sixth Schedules address administration of Scheduled Areas and specified tribal areas.

## 2. Core Notes

### A. Different types of oaths

- The nature of an oath varies with the **responsibilities attached to the office**. Oaths share common phrases but also contain office-specific commitments.
- **Minister — two oaths:** A Union or State Minister takes (1) an **oath of office** and (2) an **oath of secrecy**.
- **Why secrecy?** Ministers may receive confidential information concerning law and order, national security and international affairs. The teacher gives the example of a Defence Minister knowing sensitive information about the location of nuclear weapons.
- **Minister’s oath of office:** Includes true faith and allegiance to the Constitution, upholding India’s sovereignty and integrity, faithfully discharging ministerial duties, and doing right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and law, **without fear or favour, affection or ill-will**. This expresses impartiality and integrity in exercising public power.
- **Minister’s oath of secrecy:** A Minister promises not to directly or indirectly communicate or reveal matters that come to their consideration or knowledge as Minister, except where disclosure is required for the due discharge of ministerial duties.
- The oath of office and oath of secrecy are the same in substance for Union and State Ministers.
- **Transparency debate:** The teacher questions whether an oath of secrecy should be replaced by an oath of transparency in an age demanding open, accountable government. The Second Administrative Reforms Commission (Second ARC) recommended replacing the oath of secrecy with an oath of transparency.
- **Candidates and elected legislators:** The oath/affirmation for candidates for Parliament and State Legislatures is substantially the same, with the relevant House/State wording changed. The oath for elected members also contains common commitments to the Constitution and India’s sovereignty and integrity.
- **High Court judges:** Their oath is separately provided; it includes faithfully performing duties to the best of their ability, knowledge and judgment, and upholding the Constitution.
- **President, Vice-President and Governor:** Their oaths are provided in specific constitutional Articles rather than in the Third Schedule: President — **Article 60**; Vice-President — **Article 69**; Governor — **Article 159**.
- The President’s oath includes faithfully discharging the duties of the office. The teacher notes that similar wording appears in the oaths of elected MPs and State legislators.
- **Why different oaths?** The responsibilities of different offices require common constitutional commitments as well as specific phrases suited to each office.

### B. Fourth Schedule — Allocation of seats in the Rajya Sabha

- The **Fourth Schedule** allocates seats to States and certain Union Territories in the Rajya Sabha (Council of States).
- **United States comparison:** The US Senate has 100 members; each of the 50 States sends two Senators, irrespective of population or territorial size. This is **equal representation of States**.
- **India’s system:** India does not follow equal State representation in the Rajya Sabha. Representation is **unequal and broadly population-based**.
- Examples cited in the lecture: Uttar Pradesh — 31 seats; Goa — 1; Gujarat — 11; Haryana — 5; Kerala — 5. **[DATED - verify current]**
- Rajya Sabha representatives of a State are elected by the **elected members of that State’s Legislative Assembly**; detailed election procedure is deferred by the teacher.
- **Punchhi Commission (2007):** The Justice M.M. Punchhi Commission recommended that India consider adopting equal representation of States in the Upper House, as in the US Senate. The teacher defers discussion of its merits and drawbacks.

- The Fifth Schedule is linked to **Article 244(1)**.

- The Sixth Schedule deals with the **administration of tribal areas in four specified north-eastern States**.
- The four States identified are **Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram**.

## 3. Teacher’s Warnings and Traps

- Do not confuse the oath of office with the oath of secrecy: a Minister takes **both**.
- The oath of secrecy is not the same as permission to disclose all confidential information; the stated exception is disclosure required for the due discharge of ministerial duties.
- Do not assume that the President’s, Vice-President’s and Governor’s oaths are all located in the Third Schedule; their oaths are in separate constitutional Articles.
- Do not confuse the US system of equal State representation in the Senate with India’s population-based, unequal Rajya Sabha representation.
- Remember the **four States** covered by the Sixth Schedule: Assam, Meghalaya, Tripura and Mizoram.
