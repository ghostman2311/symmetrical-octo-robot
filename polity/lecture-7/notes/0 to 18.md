**One-line summary:** Article 368 gives Parliament constituent power to amend the Constitution, usually through a special majority in each House, with State ratification required for specified amendments.

## 1. Core Notes

### A. Background and constitutional flexibility

- The teacher connects this topic to the previous lecture: India's Constitution is relatively flexible and less rigid.
- Lecture-era comparison: approximately **105 amendments in India over 70 years**, against **27 amendments in the US over more than 200 years**. [DATED - verify current]
- The principal constitutional amendment procedure is given in **Article 368**.

### B. Parliament's constituent power

- Parliament exercises **constituent power**, similar to the Constituent Assembly's power when framing the Constitution.
- Under Article 368(1), Parliament may amend the Constitution through:
  - **Addition** — inserting provisions.
  - **Variation** — modifying provisions.
  - **Repeal** — removing provisions.
- Parliament has the principal role. State Legislatures participate only in certain specified amendments.

### C. Who can introduce a Constitutional Amendment Bill?

- A Constitutional Amendment Bill can be introduced in either House of Parliament.
- It may be introduced by:
  - A **Minister**; or
  - A **Private Member** (an MP who is not a Minister).
- A Bill introduced by a Private Member is called a **Private Member's Bill**. This applies to Constitutional Amendment Bills as well as other Bills.

### D. Special majority under Article 368(2)

The Bill must satisfy **both conditions in each House separately**:

1. **Majority of the total membership** of that House.
2. **Majority of not less than two-thirds of members present and voting** in that House.

**Both conditions are compulsory — not either/or.**

Teacher's numerical illustration:
- If 100 members are present and voting, approximately two-thirds must support the Bill.
- But satisfying this threshold alone is insufficient if the votes in favour do not also exceed half of the House's total membership.

[+] For 100 members present and voting, the minimum whole number satisfying two-thirds is 67, not 66.

### E. President's assent

- Once the Bill is passed separately by both Houses with the required majority, it is presented to the President.
- Article 368(2) states that the President **shall give assent**.
- The teacher emphasises that the President has no discretion to withhold assent once the constitutional requirements are fulfilled.

### F. Role of States and the US comparison

- The teacher states that State Legislatures have a limited role in India's amendment process, but some amendments involve them.
- The special-majority procedure discussed above applies to the overwhelming majority of constitutional provisions.
- The teacher contrasts this with the US, where amendments require ratification by three-fourths of the States. [ + ] The US Constitution permits ratification through State Legislatures or State conventions, depending on the method specified by Congress.

## 7. Additions (not from lecture)

These points are marked [+] in the core notes.

- **[+] Minimum votes in the numerical illustration:** Two-thirds of 100 is 66⅔; since votes are whole numbers, at least **67 votes** are required to satisfy that limb. This corrects the teacher's approximate figure of 66.
- **[+] Indian State ratification:** Under the proviso to Article 368(2), amendments affecting specified federal provisions require ratification by the Legislatures of **not less than half of the States**, before presentation to the President. These include changes concerning the election of the President, specified Union–State powers, the Supreme Court and High Courts, legislative relations, the Seventh Schedule, State representation in Parliament and Article 368 itself.
- **[+] US ratification method:** Article V of the US Constitution allows ratification by the legislatures of three-fourths of the States **or** by conventions in three-fourths of the States, according to the method Congress specifies. The teacher's reference to State Legislatures alone is therefore simplified.
