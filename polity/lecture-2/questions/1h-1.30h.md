## 5. Mains Practice Questions

### Question 1 — Criticism + Qualification

**Q1. Explain the criticism that formal equality before law is inadequate in a society marked by unequal starting conditions. What arguments qualify or counter this criticism?**

**Model Answer (maximum 250 words)**

**Introduction:** Formal equality requires equal application of law, while substantive equality asks whether people can genuinely enjoy equal opportunities despite structural disadvantages.

**Body**

**Criticism — Unequal starting points:** Treating an elephant and a cheetah as equal by making them start together does not create a genuinely equal contest.

**Qualification — Corrective measures:** A special concession, such as the elephant's 50-metre head start in the lecture's illustration, can reduce unequal starting conditions.

**Qualification — Equal criminal treatment:** The teacher also maintains that the same criminal offence should not attract different treatment merely because the offender belongs to a different caste, religion or economic class.

**Balance — Substantive outcome:** Differential treatment may therefore be justified to achieve real equality, while equal application of criminal law remains an important part of the Rule of Law.

**Conclusion:** Formal equality is necessary but not sufficient. Substantive equality may require corrective concessions, without abandoning equal application of criminal law.

**Keywords to hit:** Formal equality, substantive equality, unequal starting points, corrective concessions, Rule of Law, equal treatment.

**Common mistake:** Equating substantive equality with arbitrary or unlimited preferential treatment.

### Question 2 — Concept Contrast

**Q2. What is the difference between ordinary courts and quasi-judicial forums in the administration of law?**

**Model Answer (maximum 150 words)**

**Introduction:** Ordinary courts form the regular judicial hierarchy, while quasi-judicial forums are specialised bodies that perform adjudicatory functions in particular fields.

**Body**

- **Ordinary courts:** The lecture identifies the Supreme Court, High Courts, district courts and subordinate courts as the regular court structure.
- **Specialised jurisdiction:** The Central Administrative Tribunal is an example of a specialised tribunal.
- **Technical adjudication:** The Income Tax Appellate Tribunal deals with tax appeals.
- **Other forums:** Consumer dispute forums and Information Commissions also perform adjudicatory functions in their respective areas.

**Conclusion:** Ordinary courts remain central to the administration of justice, but modern governance also relies on specialised quasi-judicial forums to handle particular categories of disputes.

**Keywords to hit:** Ordinary courts, quasi-judicial forums, tribunals, CAT, specialised adjudication, Information Commissions.

**Common mistake:** Treating every quasi-judicial forum as an ordinary court.

### Question 3 — Quoted Statement + Discuss

**Q3. “Equality before law is a central principle, but constitutional and diplomatic immunities create limited exceptions to ordinary criminal jurisdiction.” Discuss.**

**Model Answer (maximum 150 words)**

**Introduction:** Equality before law requires equal application of legal standards, but some office-holders and diplomatic representatives enjoy specific immunities.

**Body**

**Constitutional exception:** Article 361 protects the President and Governors from criminal proceedings being instituted or continued during their term of office.

**Diplomatic exception:** The lecture identifies foreign ambassadors and high commissioners as enjoying immunity from criminal prosecution in the receiving State.

**Continuing principle:** The teacher's examples show that immunity is a specific legal exception; it does not mean that ordinary individuals are generally exempt from criminal law.

**Qualification:** The teacher questions the rationale and possible misuse of Article 361, while treating diplomatic immunity as a separate recognised exception.

**Conclusion:** Immunities qualify the general operation of equality before law, and their scope and justification must be assessed within the relevant constitutional or international framework.

**Keywords to hit:** Equality before law, Article 361, diplomatic immunity, criminal jurisdiction, constitutional office, legal exception.

**Common mistake:** Assuming that immunity means the underlying conduct can never be examined or acted upon by any State.

### Question 4 — Critical Evaluation

**Q4. Does Article 361's immunity for the President and Governors demonstrate mindless imitation of British constitutional practice? Explain critically.**

**Model Answer (maximum 250 words)**

**Introduction:** Article 361 grants specific protections to the President and Governors while they hold office. The lecture presents a constitutional scholar's criticism that this provision reflects uncritical borrowing from British practice.

**Body**

**Borrowed rationale:** The criticism is that the British Crown enjoyed comparable immunity because British courts were historically treated as institutions of the Crown.

**Different constitutional foundations:** The argument is that Indian courts and the President are both creations of the Constitution, so the British rationale cannot automatically be transplanted.

**Risk of misuse:** The lecture raises the possibility that a politician facing prosecution could be appointed Governor to delay criminal proceedings during the tenure.

**Hypothetical test:** The speaker argues that a proposal to introduce such immunity through a new constitutional amendment today would likely face strong public and institutional opposition. **[teacher's view]**

**Qualification:** Article 361 is an existing constitutional provision and its text must be understood as a defined, tenure-linked protection; criticism of its policy rationale does not itself remove it.

**Conclusion:** The lecture gives substantial grounds to question the rationale and possible misuse of the immunity, but “mindless imitation” remains a critical assessment rather than a settled legal conclusion.

**Keywords to hit:** Article 361, British Crown, constitutional borrowing, judicial independence, abuse of immunity, constitutional accountability.

**Common mistake:** Presenting the scholar's criticism as if it were the constitutional text or a judicial holding.
