## 4. Mains Practice Questions

### Q1. Explain the criticism that legislative freedom of speech may weaken accountability. What arguments qualify this criticism?
**Model answer (up to 250 words)**

**Intro:** Legislative privilege protects speech in the House so representatives can debate public issues without fear of court proceedings.

**Body — Criticism:**  
- **Accountability concern:** A member may make defamatory or contemptuous remarks in the House without being proceeded against in court for those words.

**Qualification:**  
- **Purpose:** The privilege enables members to speak freely on behalf of constituents.
- **Limited setting:** The protection discussed applies to speech in the House, not outside remarks.
- **Internal control:** The Speaker and House may expunge unparliamentary remarks and initiate disciplinary action.
- **Constitutional basis:** Article 105(2) protects parliamentary speech and votes; Article 194(2) provides the State Legislature counterpart.

**Conclusion:** Legislative privilege is a functional protection for representative debate, not an unrestricted personal licence. Its legitimacy depends on the House exercising its own disciplinary powers responsibly.

**Keywords to hit:** Legislative privilege, representation, Article 105(2), Article 194(2), internal discipline, accountability.  
**Common mistake:** Treating parliamentary immunity as applying to every statement made by an MP, including outside the House.

### Q2. What is the difference between court-proceeding immunity and internal legislative discipline?
**Model answer (up to 150 words)**

**Intro:** Court-proceeding immunity and internal discipline are separate aspects of legislative privilege.

**Body**
- **Court immunity:** Article 105(2) protects an MP from court proceedings for anything said or any vote given in Parliament or its committees.
- **State counterpart:** Article 194(2) provides corresponding protection to State Legislature members.
- **Internal discipline:** The House can control its own proceedings, including expunging words and taking disciplinary action.
- **Practical distinction:** A member may be shielded from court action for protected speech but still be subject to the House’s rules.

**Conclusion:** Immunity limits external court proceedings; it does not remove the House’s authority over its own members.

**Keywords to hit:** Court immunity, Article 105, Article 194, House authority, expunction, discipline.  
**Common mistake:** Assuming court immunity prevents the Speaker or House from taking action.

### Q3. “Legislative privilege protects free debate, but the House remains responsible for maintaining order.” Discuss.
**Model answer (up to 150 words)**

**Intro:** Legislative privilege aims to protect representative debate while preserving orderly proceedings.

**Body — Protection:**  
- **Free expression:** Members need to convey constituents’ views frankly.
- **Constitutional guarantee:** Article 105(2) protects speech and votes in Parliament from court proceedings.

**Qualification:**  
- **Order:** The Speaker may have unparliamentary remarks expunged.
- **Discipline:** The House may suspend a member or refer conduct to its Ethics Committee, as described in the lecture.
- **State legislatures:** Article 194(2) provides a parallel protection for their members.

**Conclusion:** The privilege and the House’s disciplinary authority operate together: one protects debate, the other maintains order.

**Keywords to hit:** Free debate, representation, privilege, Speaker, expunction, discipline.  
**Common mistake:** Saying privilege means there can be no consequences of any kind.

### Q4. Does parliamentary privilege prove that legislators are above the law? Explain critically.
**Model answer (up to 250 words)**

**Intro:** Parliamentary privilege is a constitutionally recognized protection for legislative functioning; it does not make legislators generally immune from law.

**Body**
- **Limited protection:** Article 105(2) concerns court proceedings for speech or votes in Parliament and its committees.
- **Location and function:** The lecture confines the example to speech on the floor of the House while performing legislative duties.
- **Outside conduct:** The teacher distinguishes protected House speech from statements made outside the House, where ordinary legal rules may apply.
- **House control:** The Speaker and House can address unparliamentary language through expunction and internal discipline.
- **State legislatures:** Article 194(2) provides the corresponding protection for State Legislature members.

**Conclusion:** Privilege creates a narrow functional immunity, not general lawlessness. Its limits—especially the protected setting and the House’s internal disciplinary authority—are central to understanding it.

**Keywords to hit:** Parliamentary privilege, limited immunity, Article 105(2), House proceedings, ordinary law, discipline.  
**Common mistake:** Confusing a member’s protected legislative speech with blanket immunity from prosecution for all conduct.
