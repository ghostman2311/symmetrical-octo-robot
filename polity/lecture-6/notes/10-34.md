# Sources of the Indian Constitution — UPSC Notes

This lecture covers the First-Past-the-Post system and the constitutional features India borrowed from different countries. The key exam focus is remembering the correct country–feature associations and understanding the limitations of FPTP.

## 1. First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) System

India follows the First-Past-the-Post (FPTP) system for direct elections to the Lok Sabha and State Legislative Assemblies.

- The candidate who receives the highest number of votes wins.
- The winner does not need to secure more than 50% of the total votes.
- This is also called the plurality system.

Example: If 100 voters choose among 10 candidates, a candidate receiving 11 votes can win if every other candidate receives fewer votes.

### Limitation

A candidate may win despite receiving support from only a small proportion of the electorate. This raises questions about the representative's electoral legitimacy.

### Run-off Election System

Some countries, including France in its presidential elections, use a two-round system:

1. Candidates contest in the first round.
2. If nobody secures the required majority, a second round is held between the leading candidates.
3. The winner of the second round is elected.

Why doesn't India generally use this system?

- A second round would increase election costs and logistical requirements.
- Since many Indian constituencies have several candidates, a second round could be required in a large number of constituencies.
- It would increase the time and administrative burden of elections.

Important: A run-off can improve the winner's majority support in the final round, but it does not guarantee that the winner was the first-choice candidate of most voters.

## 2. Sources of the Indian Constitution

The Constitution borrowed and adapted provisions from several countries. Memorise these country–feature pairs for UPSC.

| Country / Source    | Features borrowed                                                                                                                             |
| ------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| United Kingdom      | Parliamentary system, rule of law, cabinet system, single citizenship, bicameralism, legislative procedure and FPTP                           |
| USA                 | Fundamental Rights, judicial review, independence of judiciary, impeachment of President, removal of judges and office of Vice-President      |
| Canada              | Federal system with a strong Centre, residuary powers with Centre, appointment of Governors by Centre, advisory jurisdiction of Supreme Court |
| Ireland             | Directive Principles of State Policy (DPSP), method of election of President, nomination of members to Rajya Sabha                            |
| Japan               | Procedure established by law                                                                                                                  |
| South Africa        | Constitutional amendment procedure, election of Rajya Sabha members                                                                           |
| USSR (Soviet Union) | Fundamental Duties, ideals of social, economic and political justice in the Preamble                                                          |
| France              | Republic, ideals of liberty, equality and fraternity                                                                                          |
| Australia           | Concurrent List, freedom of trade and commerce, joint sitting of Parliament                                                                   |
| Germany (Weimar)    | Suspension of Fundamental Rights during Emergency                                                                                             |

### Important: Government of India Act, 1935

It was a major source of the Indian Constitution's administrative and structural framework.

Important features derived from it include:

- Federal scheme
- Office of Governor
- Public Service Commissions
- Emergency provisions
- Administrative details
- Judiciary-related framework

## 3. Important Constitutional Concepts

Judicial Review (USA): Power of the judiciary to examine legislative and executive actions for constitutional validity. Unconstitutional laws or actions can be struck down.

Strong Centre (Canada): India has a federal structure, but the Union Government has comparatively stronger powers than the States.

Residuary Powers (Canada): Subjects not mentioned in the Union, State or Concurrent Lists fall within the legislative jurisdiction of Parliament. See Article 248.

Advisory Jurisdiction (Canada): Under Article 143, the President can refer questions of law or fact to the Supreme Court for its opinion.

Procedure Established by Law (Japan): Article 21 of the Japanese Constitution is not the relevant provision; in India, the phrase appears in Article 21. It means deprivation of life or personal liberty must follow a procedure established by law.

Republic (France): India is a republic because its head of state, the President, is elected rather than inheriting the position.

## 4. Important Corrections and Exam Traps

- Social, economic and political justice in the Preamble is associated with the USSR, not South Africa.
- Fundamental Duties were inspired by the USSR and added through the 42nd Constitutional Amendment, 1976.
- Procedure established by law came from Japan; do not confuse it with due process of law.
- The American Constitution's Bill of Rights inspired India's Fundamental Rights, but the two are not identical.
- India follows FPTP for Lok Sabha and State Assembly elections; it does not generally require a candidate to win 50% of votes.

## Practice Questions

1. Which electoral system does India follow for Lok Sabha elections?
2. Does a candidate need more than 50% votes to win under FPTP?
3. Which country inspired the idea of a strong Centre in a federal system?
4. Which country inspired the Directive Principles of State Policy?
5. From where did India borrow the concept of judicial review?
6. Which country inspired the procedure established by law?
7. Fundamental Duties were inspired by which country?
8. Which country inspired the amendment procedure of the Indian Constitution?
9. Under which Article does the Supreme Court exercise advisory jurisdiction?
10. Why is India called a republic?

## Answer Key

1. First-Past-the-Post.
2. No.
3. Canada.
4. Ireland.
5. USA.
6. Japan.
7. USSR.
8. South Africa.
9. Article 143.
10. Because the President, the head of state, is elected and does not hold office by hereditary succession.
