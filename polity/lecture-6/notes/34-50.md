## Corrections to remember for UPSC

A few points in the lecture need more precise wording:

- Public employment: Equality of opportunity in public employment is specifically guaranteed by Article 16, not the definition of a republic itself.
- Suspension of Fundamental Rights: It is not accurate to say that all Fundamental Rights are suspended during a National Emergency. Articles 358 and 359 deal with different aspects, and Articles 20 and 21 receive special protection.
- Unwritten Constitution: The better term for the UK is uncodified constitution. Many constitutional rules are written in statutes and judicial decisions, but they are not consolidated into one supreme constitutional document.
- Constitutional conventions: They are generally political or constitutional practices, not automatically legally enforceable rules.

# Indian Constitution — Republic, Sovereignty and Written Constitution

## 1. Meaning of Republic

A **Republic** is a form of government in which the head of state is elected, directly or indirectly, rather than holding office through hereditary succession.

### Three ideas associated with a republic

1. **Elected head of state:** India has an elected President, unlike a hereditary monarch.
2. **Equal opportunity in public offices:** Citizens can compete for public employment subject to eligibility conditions. Article 16 guarantees equality of opportunity in matters of public employment.
3. **Popular sovereignty:** Ultimate political authority originates from the people.

**Important:** Equality of opportunity in public employment is a constitutional guarantee under Article 16; it is not the formal definition of a republic.

## 2. Sovereignty and Popular Sovereignty

- Sovereignty means the supreme authority of a state to govern itself without being subordinate to an external power.
- Before independence, India was not a sovereign state.
- After independence, India acquired sovereign authority.
- The Preamble begins with **“We, the People of India”**, expressing the idea that the Constitution derives its authority from the people.

### Popular sovereignty

The people are the ultimate source of political authority. Through elections, they choose representatives who exercise governmental power under the Constitution.

Think of it as a trust:

- People are the source of authority.
- Government exercises public power on their behalf.
- Government must act within constitutional limits and remain accountable.

**UPSC point:** The government does not possess unlimited power simply because it is elected. Its authority is constitutionally limited.

## 3. Sources of the Indian Constitution — Additional Points

| Country                       | Features borrowed                                                           |
| ----------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Australia                     | Concurrent List, joint sitting of Parliament, freedom of trade and commerce |
| Germany (Weimar Constitution) | Suspension of certain Fundamental Rights during an emergency                |

### Emergency provisions — Important distinction

India has three types of emergencies:

| Article | Emergency           | Key point                                        |
| ------- | ------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| 352     | National Emergency  | War, external aggression or armed rebellion      |
| 356     | President's Rule    | Failure of constitutional machinery in a state   |
| 360     | Financial Emergency | Threat to financial stability or credit of India |

**Fundamental Rights during emergencies:**

- Article 358 deals with the effect of a National Emergency on Article 19. It applies only when the emergency is declared on grounds of war or external aggression.
- Article 359 allows the President to suspend the right to move courts for enforcement of specified Fundamental Rights, subject to constitutional safeguards.
- Articles 20 and 21 cannot be suspended through an order under Article 359.
- Article 356 does not itself suspend Fundamental Rights.

**Remember:** The emergency-related suspension of certain Fundamental Rights is commonly listed as borrowed from the Weimar Constitution of Germany.

## 4. Written vs. Unwritten Constitution

The United States is generally recognised as having the first modern codified written constitution.

India also has a written, codified Constitution.

The UK is commonly described as having an unwritten Constitution, but **uncodified Constitution** is the more accurate expression.

| Written / Codified                                                | Unwritten / Uncodified                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Constitutional rules are consolidated in a principal document.    | Constitutional rules are spread across different sources.                               |
| Greater reliance on a codified constitutional text.               | Greater reliance on statutes, conventions, judicial decisions and practices.            |
| Amendments generally follow prescribed constitutional procedures. | Constitutional arrangements can evolve through different legal and political processes. |

### Constitutional conventions

Conventions are established political practices or accepted ways of conducting government.

- They help institutions function smoothly.
- They provide flexibility and adaptability.
- They are generally not legally enforceable in the same way as statutory provisions.
- Different interpretations of conventions can create uncertainty.

### Difference is one of degree, not absolute

- A written constitution can also develop conventions.
- An uncodified constitution can contain many written laws.
- Therefore, the distinction is about the extent of codification, not whether anything is written or unwritten.

**Example:** The UK Constitution includes statutes, judicial decisions, conventions and practices, rather than one consolidated constitutional document.

## Quick Revision

- Republic: Elected head of state, not hereditary.
- Article 16: Equality of opportunity in public employment.
- Popular sovereignty: Ultimate authority originates from the people.
- Article 352: National Emergency.
- Article 356: President's Rule.
- Article 360: Financial Emergency.
- Article 358: Effect on Article 19 during specified National Emergencies.
- Article 359: Suspension of the right to move courts for specified Fundamental Rights.
- Australia: Concurrent List, joint sitting, trade and commerce.
- Weimar Germany: Emergency-related suspension of certain Fundamental Rights.
- UK: Uncodified Constitution.
- Difference between written and unwritten constitutions: One of degree, not absolute.
